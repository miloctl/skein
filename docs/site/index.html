🧵 Skein — internal team platform

Many strands. One formation.

The coordination harness for our strike team — humans and AI agents drafting off each other, earning turns at the front, receipts at every checkpoint.

A skein is two things: a coil of yarn — many strands wound so they pull as one — and geese flying in V-formation, where the lead rotates and every bird's lift carries the one behind it. Skein is that, as software: engagements, milestones, tasks, blockers, questions, decisions, standups, intake triage, a knowledge base, and a team calendar — shared between people and their agents, with one write path, provenance on everything, and an approval gate agents have to earn their way past.

0 API keys required — deterministic core, models are an upgrade
39 agent tools, 1 write path — agents use the same services humans do
receipts on every verdict — row IDs and numbers behind each claim
How it works

From “someone should write that down” to “here's the receipt.”

Capture

Press ⌘K anywhere and type. blocked on vendor contract becomes a blocker. q: who owns DNS? becomes a question. promised: demo by Friday lands in the commitment ledger. Rule-based routing — no model in the loop, no excuse not to capture. Same capture from the CLI, Slack, and MCP.

The record

Everything captured becomes a real record with provenance: who wrote it, whether they were human or agent, and an activity-log entry. Decisions carry a half-life and go stale on schedule instead of silently rotting. Full-text search covers all of it.

The rhythm

Skein runs the week so nobody has to remember to. A drafted Monday plan lands in the review inbox — approved, not imposed. The daily digest opens with a shared joke and goes straight-faced when something's escalated. Blockers escalate on impact-based deadlines, and the three-day-old ones get a funeral when they finally die.

Agents earn trust

Agents hold an authority level per entity type — most start at review: every write is a proposal a human approves. Approval streaks build trust; a strong streak suggests promotion, and a human still flips the switch.

Insights

A findings engine watches the record with deterministic rules and speaks at most three times a week, severity-ordered, receipts attached. Below its sample floors it says nothing — silence is a valid output.

What's built

Every feature, honestly described.

Capture & the record

Five ways in, one record out — and the record doesn't rot.

  • ⌘K quick capture — q: question · todo: task · blocked… blocker · decision: decision · promised: commitment · else note. Palette, REST, CLI, Slack, MCP.
  • Standups that file blockers — blocker text auto-becomes a real blocker in the register.
  • Decisions with a half-life — review_by dates, a daily stale sweep, supersede chains instead of contradictions.
  • Questions with owners — assigned, notified, aged; open past five days gets named in the digest.
  • Full-text search over every entity, keyless.

Engagements & portfolio

The strike-team unit of work, with health calls that show their math.

  • Playbook kickoffs — templates spin up milestones, tasks, rituals, and the lessons paid for last time.
  • Intake with a spine — submit → RICE-lite score → accept/defer/decline with a reason; what-if staffing answers “if we take this, Dana hits 130%.”
  • Health with receipts — R/Y/G per engagement, every verdict lists why.
  • Slip forecast, labeled honestly — our own slip history projected forward, basis shown.
  • Commitment ledger & exec readout — external promises tracked to kept/missed; one-command stakeholder readout.

The weekly rhythm

The week runs itself so Monday morning doesn't run you.

  • The commitment line — committed-vs-done and kept-% per week. The team's word, measured.
  • Auto-drafted Monday plan — five tasks max per person, filed as a proposal. Approved, not imposed.
  • Daily digest — deterministic, 07:00 UTC; drops the joke whenever something is escalated.
  • Escalation & funerals — critical blockers escalate in 2 hours, low in 72; three-day survivors get a funeral. 🪦
  • Team pulse — seasons, standup chains, blocker speedruns. No individual leaderboards, ever.

Agents as teammates — the trust system

Agents don't get autonomy. They earn it — one approved proposal at a time.

forbidden→ review · the default→ notify→ autonomous
  • Same tools, same rules — 39 agent tools wrap the exact services humans use. No side door; every agent write carries origin: agent provenance.
  • The authority matrix — per agent, per entity type, enforced in the tool gate — including MCP. Only humans can change a level; an agent asking to promote itself is refused.
  • Trust scores that suggest, never decide — five approvals in a row suggests promotion. A human flips the switch, every time.
  • Delegation with a sponsor — agent work always has a named human accountable for it.
  • Agent inboxes — delegated tasks, questions, and rejected proposals with the reviewer's notes — agents learn from the no, not just the yes.
  • Cross-thread memory — remember and recall across conversations, keyless.
trust: code-agent on task — 5/5 approved · streak 5 suggestion: consider promoting to autonomous (a human decides)

The review flywheel & evals

Every approve and reject makes the system measurably better.

  • Review inbox — approve/reject with notes; claim-locked; failed applies never lose the proposal.
  • Review analytics — approval rates and latency per entity and proposer; recent rejection reasons.
  • A self-growing eval corpus — thumbs and corrections become labeled data.
  • skein eval — replays the capture classifier against the team's own corrections; exits nonzero on regressions.

Insights & findings

Findings are sentences with receipts. Charts are the evidence behind them.

  • Deterministic rules — MTTR regressions, aging WIP, slipping commitment lines, review-queue stalls, decision decay, token anomalies — each with explicit sample floors.
  • Receipts stored at fire time — the row IDs and numbers that triggered each finding.
  • Small-n discipline — medians over means, n printed everywhere, no %-claims under n=8.
  • Max three findings a week — severity-ordered, in the digest. Silence is a valid output.
  • Anti-surveillance, structurally — person-level data plans the future; judging the past is team-rolled only.

The developer loop

Skein meets you in the terminal, the commit message, and your other agents.

  • Per-teammate API keys — hashed, shown once; they authenticate and attribute.
  • Stdlib-only CLI — skein capture|standup|my-day|tasks|week|eval|context.
  • Git trailers & CI webhook — Closes-Task: #12 closes the task; a red main build files a blocker, green resolves it.
  • MCP server — your other AI agents join the platform against the same DB.
  • The context pack — a versioned org-brain; skein context --write AGENTS.md makes Skein the context supplier to every agent you run.

Ops & trust

Boring on purpose: one box, one write path, backups you can count.

  • Keyless-first, permanently — mock provider end-to-end; a signed-in Ollama daemon gets a real model for free.
  • Provenance on every write — origin + author + activity log, both write paths, no exceptions.
  • Backups that leave the box — atomic daily backups (14 kept) + off-box mirror (30 kept).
  • Append-only migrations, idempotent scheduler — every job safe to re-run.
  • A security model stated plainly — documented limits instead of pretended ones.
Principles

The rules Skein keeps.

Principles are cheap when they're prose. These are enforced in code.

  • Provenance on every write.Human or agent, REST or tool — every record says who and what wrote it.
  • Receipts, shown.If Skein can't show its math, it doesn't make the claim.
  • No individual leaderboards.Person-level data plans the future — it never scores the past.
  • Silence is a valid output.Rules stay quiet below their sample floors. Early emptiness is honesty.
  • Keyless-first, forever.Models make it better; they never gate it.
  • Agents earn autonomy.Default is review. Trust suggests; humans grant. Self-promotion is refused by the gate.
